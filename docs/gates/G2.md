## 1. Summary and recommendation

**Recommendation: proceed to M3, subject to brief items 2 and 4.** The B2 instruments (the C# WinEvent listener and the in-page DOM mutation timeline, on one QPC timebase) meet the G2 rule on `windows-2025`:

- 52 valid runs of each gating canary (260 in all), with **0 signature failures**;
- INCONCLUSIVE in 0 of 260 gating attempts (limit 5%), and in 0 of 450 attempts in the G2 run;
- every job's listener probe installed six of six hook ranges, and every attempt's listener became ready, passed its UIA self-check and produced events.

The zero failures rest on the operational signature definitions, which decide what counts as a B2 match. Claude fixed them before the G2 runs (DR-0053). The gate review then narrowed the identity rule, which had let the LiveSetting fallback attribute a region's descendant text to the region. Re-scored from the raw evidence under the narrowed rules, all 260 gating verdicts and all 190 record-only traces are unchanged. The definitions go to the owner for ratification (brief item 2, P12).

The NVDA-absent K6e signatures agree with P9's post-load boundary for polite regions: one-rAF fills reach the platform as a populated insertion (0 of 10 with a separate update) and fills from 50 ms as a separate update (50 of 50). Timer fills under 50 ms were not tested (brief item 3). With NVDA running, the signatures matched as without it (on/off diagnostic, 100 of 100 against 99 of 99). The diagnostic also changes how the canary is activated, so it cannot isolate NVDA's effect on its own (§2.4).

A "separate update" is a LIVEREGIONCHANGED or IA2 text event attributed to the region after its DOM insertion (DR-0053, P12). The K6e 0 ms condition fills in the same task as the insertion, so the timeline records it exactly like a populated insertion. It is a control, not a test of P9's boundary.

**K7 order.** In the DOM the canary sets the text and then calls `focus()` in one task, so the text precedes the focus move by construction (20 of 20 in each of K7a and K7b). On the platform, Chrome raised the update and the focus move in one batch, within about 0.6 ms. IA2 TEXT_INSERTED on `#live` preceded EVENT_OBJECT_FOCUS in 20 of 20 for each canary. LIVEREGIONCHANGED on `#live` followed EVENT_OBJECT_FOCUS in 20 of 20 for each. Neither channel shows the order as an observed property.

**P9 against B2.** The NVDA-absent signatures agree with NVDA's speech (G1 and the M2 pilot) and with P9's boundary as tested:

- One-rAF fills look like populated insertions on the platform: the region's SHOW, and no live-region or text event, in 0 of 10 (0 of 20 with the pilot). The region's SHOW came 1.7–2.8 ms after each rAF fill, so the fill was serialised with the insertion.
- Fills from 50 ms reach the platform as a separate update, 50 of 50.
- No timer fill under 50 ms was tested. The pre-registered rule graded any fill within one 60 Hz frame (16.7 ms) as the same frame. A timer fill at 5–16 ms could land after the insertion is serialised, which took 6–14 ms, so the time threshold is not validated for timer fills (inferred from the timings).
- From timeline version 2, the code reads "one rAF" directly. A fill made in a `requestAnimationFrame` callback within one frame is the same frame. Any other fill under 50 ms routes to REVIEW. The G2 run's version 1 records keep the pre-registered threshold. P9's REVIEW band, the 350 ms pre-load boundary and roles other than polite remain untested.

**Chrome's serialisation window on the platform.** In the G2 run the region's SHOW arrived a median 8.8–11.9 ms after the DOM insertion (range 6.7–13.8 ms across the K6e delays). The fill's IA2 TEXT_INSERTED arrived as follows (n = 10 each; the pilot gave the same within 2 ms):

| Fill delay | TEXT_INSERTED after the DOM insertion, median (range) | After the fill (difference of medians) |
|---|---|---|
| 50 ms | 166.3 ms (164.1–167.7) | about 116 ms |
| 100 ms | 165.8 ms (163.7–167.6) | about 65 ms |
| 150 ms | 166.8 ms (164.8–167.7) | about 16 ms |
| 250 ms | 260.4 ms (258.4–262.0) | about 10 ms |
| 500 ms | 510.6 ms (508.3–513.4) | about 10 ms |

These timings are consistent with Chrome serialising the insertion at once and holding a change that comes within about 150 ms of that serialisation until the window closes (inferred from the timings, not from Chrome's source). On that reading, a region looks populated on the platform only when it is filled before its insertion is serialised, which is P9's boundary. DR-0037's premise, that any fill inside the window can look like a populated insertion, did not hold in the tested condition: a polite region inserted after load, followed by one change after a quiet period of at least about 450 ms. Other conditions, such as several rapid changes, were not tested.

- **Systematic differences.** These are events on one element present in at least 90% of one leg's attempts and at most 10% of the other's. There were two, both seen only with NVDA absent: EVENT_OBJECT_STATECHANGE on the "Start canary" button in every attempt (about 45 ms after activation), and EVENT_OBJECT_STATECHANGE on `main` in every K5 attempt, at the canary's own DOM change. The NVDA-absent leg activates with Playwright's click and the NVDA-present leg with an OS-level Enter through NVDA. In K5, `main` is the element left under the pointer once the canary empties it. Both are inferred to be pointer-state changes from the click (pressed or hover; the listener does not record state bits). The signature events were otherwise the same.
- **Confound.** The diagnostic changes NVDA presence and the activation method together, so it cannot isolate NVDA's effect on its own.
- **Speech.** With the listener running, NVDA conveyed every gating canary (100 of 100 under the G1 speech matching). Speech outcomes count queued-then-cancelled text as spoken, and speech-manager cancels never reach the relay (D13, DR-0022).
- **Parity.** Tap-versus-log parity was exact per segment (160 against 160). Per NVDA run it was equal in 5 of 10 runs, and the log was 1 or 2 entries ahead in the other 5. The pilot's NVDA-present leg was equal in 7 of 10, with the log 1 or 2 ahead in 3. As in G1 the cause is inferred to be entries at the run window's start, within the wall anchor's resolution (not checked entry by entry).

- **`g2-only`:** §2.1, §2.2, §2.3 (grades, delays, separate updates, region events, the SHOW and TEXT_INSERTED timing, K7 order) and §2.6 (clock percentiles, listener and focus-read counts).
- **`onoff-only`:** §2.4 (the table, the systematic differences and speech with the listener present). Its `report.md` gives the parity figures.
- **`pilot-only`:** the pilot's K6e timing and P4 latency.

Rebuilding the G2 run's report from the bundle gives the same `report-g2.md` as the downloaded artefacts. The same code also rebuilds the G1 report from the G1 bundle unchanged.
